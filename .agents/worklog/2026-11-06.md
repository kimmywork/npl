## npli: nested closure call sites (perf)

Goal (user, 2026-11-06): a faster npli. C4 (design-calls addendum,
2026-11-05) was not started: on closer reading the evaluator's arithmetic
nodes have a slow path that calls whatever the operator cell holds, so
they can never be direct-safe, and C4's estimate did not hold.

Measurement instead: npli's syntax nodes call their children with a
literal continuation whose body calls the next child, so every C3 call
site's continuation contained a lambda or another site, which
`control-closure-call-site?` excluded. Removing that exclusion
(`frontend/direct-calls.npl control-inline-size`) lets sites nest; the
120-node size bound now counts nested sites and lambdas.

## npli: if-test folding experiment (not committed)

Tested constant-folding of `(if (< a b) ...)` when `<` is bound to the
expected arithmetic procedure. Implementation: extend `eval-try-fold-if-test`
to recognize binary comparison operators and generate a direct call to the
native procedure when the cell holds the expected value, with a runtime
check `(eq? (car cell) expected)`.

Result: fib ~−4% (0.80 s baseline, 0.77 s folded), loop within noise.
收益太小，不值得提交。The runtime check `(eq? (car cell) expected)`
itself has overhead that mostly cancels the benefit of avoiding one layer
of indirection through `eval-cell-call-2`.

Further npli optimization would require more aggressive techniques: loop
recognition with specialized codegen, bytecode compilation, or other
structural changes. The current 2-3x gap vs Guile interpreter is acceptable
for an AST-walking evaluator.
